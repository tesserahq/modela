My close friend Ray Jefferson passed away suddenly three months ago. After arranging his Singapore memorial, meeting his family, attending his US wake and military funeral, completing a major group effort of clearing his home (last Monday) and office (yesterday), and starting legal processes, I now have some time to reflect on simple preparations for what we leave behind.

Note: this is not exhaustive, does not constitute legal advice, and for differing views or questions, please consult experts.

What I realised after Ray Jefferson’s passing – part 1 of X

As soon as you can, write down, and share with at least two of the closest people in your life the following (ideally in one digital place):

• Who are the closest people to you, where are they, and how can they be contacted?
• Who are the people you trust, who can make decisions on your behalf?
• Who are your advisors? (religious, tax, financial planning, accounting, business, legal, medical).
• Where is your will? Include your assets and insurance if not in the will.
• In plain words, what are your intentions in your will? (for family, not for legal purposes).
• Where is your Lasting Power of Attorney, and who does it appoint?
• Where is your Advanced Medical Directive, and (for family, not for legal purposes) in plain words what are your medical intentions?
• What are your wishes for your body (cremation or burial, specific location)? Should organs be donated? Do you have a view on an autopsy if relevant?
• What are your wishes for a memorial, or leave it to the living to decide?
• How do people access your documents (physical and digital), home, office, or vehicles?
• What accounts, devices, and places need access, and how? (e.g., locks, safes, passwords, keys, encryption).
• Where are recent high-resolution photos of you?
• What spiritual beliefs would you want known?
• What messages do you have for others?

Additional observations:

1. You likely have too many things—clothes, books, and extra supplies. Reduce.
2. Make your wills. Ensure one exists for every jurisdiction where you have assets.
3. You likely have too many financial accounts; close unnecessary ones.
4. There’s more to consider—work and financial impacts, Wikipedia and website updates, obituaries, liabilities, disposal of assets. This list is a selection from my experience.

I hope these reflections bring peace of mind to at least one person. My next post might cover behaviours and reactions I observed during this time.
